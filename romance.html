Blurb

The synopsis that should not be read aloud at work.

Erin DeVine came to El Dorado County to keep projects on schedule. She had an M.S. from UCSB, a clipboard from Novaterra, and absolutely no patience for men who thought "cleavage" was a compliment.

Then a rugged stranger asked what she did for a living. She said she stripped the earth. He said prove it. She said get lost in an orogenous zone.

He did.

Now the mountains are rising, the overburden is coming off, and the only thing more unstable than the slope is the man who followed her into the range. There is one tent. One competent layer. And a geologist who has been taking it off — professionally — since 2003.

Warning: This novel contains plate tectonics, groundwater talk, a supply-closet scene that should not be quoted in any peer-reviewed journal, and a heroine who can identify foliation and ruin you in the same sentence.

About the author

Erin DeVine, M.S.

Erin DeVine holds a Master's in Marine Science and Geology from UC Santa Barbara. She has been a geologist at Parsons, a staff scientist at Conestoga-Rovers, and a person who kept SWCA Environmental Consultants organized.

She currently works as Project Control Coordinator at Novaterra Biological Consulting, where she keeps projects on budget, on schedule, and inside the permit.

When she is not taking rocks' clothes off for a living, she writes romance novels about geologists. She is currently working on Book Two: Strike It Rich (in the Alluvium).

She lives in El Dorado County, where the gold is real, the cleavage is geological, and the men are all overburden.
