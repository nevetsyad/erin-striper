Get Lost in My Orogenous Zone

by Erin DeVine, M.S.

Erin DeVine came to El Dorado County to keep projects on schedule. She had an M.S. from UC Santa Barbara, a clipboard from Novaterra, and absolutely no patience for men who thought "cleavage" was a compliment.

Then a rugged stranger asked what she did for a living. She said she stripped the earth. He said prove it. She said get lost in an orogenous zone.

He did.

Now the mountains are rising, the overburden is coming off, and the only thing more unstable than the slope is the man who followed her into the range. There is one tent. One competent layer. She knows hardness by touch. He has no idea what a thrust fault is until Chapter 7.

By the time the orogeny begins, there is no alignment left to maintain — only bed-load, uplift, and the kind of penetration twinning that geologists only discuss in confidence after three glasses of wine.

About the Author

Erin DeVine, M.S.

Erin DeVine holds a Master's in Marine Science and Geology from UC Santa Barbara. She has worked as a geologist at Parsons, a staff scientist at Conestoga-Rovers & Associates, and a person who kept SWCA Environmental Consultants organized.

She currently serves as Project Control Coordinator at Novaterra Biological Consulting, where she keeps projects on budget, on schedule, and inside the permit.

When she is not stripping the earth for a living, she writes romance novels about geologists. Her debut novel, Get Lost in My Orogenous Zone, was accepted by three publishers and then withdrawn when the acquisition editor realized the table of contents.

She lives in El Dorado County, where the gold is real, the cleavage is geological, the uplift is tectonic, and the men are all overburden.

This is a fake book advertisement for a fake book about a real geologist, made as a joke. Erin DeVine did not write this, star in this, or consent to a supply-closet chapter. There is no preorder. There is no ISBN. There is, however, an orogenous zone, and you were told to get lost in it.
